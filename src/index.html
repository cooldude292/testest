Solves Maxwell's equations (FDTD) on the floor plan at the real WiFi frequency, so reflections, wall shadows and interference are real. Walls use ITU-R P.2040 material data. It's a 2D slice of the floor with the 3D distance falloff folded back in; dBm assumes a typical router (about -40 dBm at 1 m). Great for finding dead zones and where to put the router.

Body

Full 3D lattice Boltzmann solve (D3Q19 + LES turbulence model) on your GPU. Forces come straight from the flow hitting the voxelised body. Streamline pulses travel at the local flow speed. The grid is coarse next to a real CFD mesh and the lattice can't reach real-world Reynolds numbers, so treat numbers as good estimates for comparing shapes, not certification data.

The flow is solved live on a 2D slice through the body with a lattice Boltzmann solver + LES turbulence model. 3D forces are estimated by applying the slice's coefficients to the body's real frontal and planform areas. Good for comparing shapes, angles and fluids. Not a substitute for a real CFD run or a real tunnel.
